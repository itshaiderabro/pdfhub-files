FGEI EST BPS-14 Preparation Package   Page 11  SUBJECT 5: PEDAGOGY (20%)  Teaching Methods · Child Development · Assessment  5.1 Bloom's Taxonomy (Revised)  l   Bloom's Taxonomy (1956, revised by Anderson & Krathwohl 2001) classifies cognitive skills into 6 levels:  Level 1 — Remember:   Recall facts: define, list, name, memorize.  Level 2 — Understand:   Explain ideas: describe, summarize, classify, paraphrase.  Level 3 — Apply:   Use knowledge in new situations: solve, use, demonstrate.  Level 4 — Analyze:   Break down information: compare, differentiate, examine.  Level 5 — Evaluate:   Make judgments: assess, critique, justify, argue.  Level 6 — Create:   Produce new work: design, construct, compose, plan.  l   HOTS   (Higher Order Thinking Skills) = Analyze, Evaluate, Create |   LOTS   = Remember, Understand, Apply  5.2 Child Development Theories  Jean Piaget (Cognitive Development):  l   • Sensorimotor (0-2 yrs): object permanence develops.  l   • Preoperational (2-7 yrs): symbolic thinking, egocentric, no conservation.  l   • Concrete Operational (7-11 yrs): logical thinking about concrete objects, conservation achieved.  l   • Formal Operational (12+ yrs): abstract reasoning, hypothetical thinking.  Lev Vygotsky (Sociocultural Theory):  l   • Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD): gap between what a learner can do alone vs. with guidance.  l   • Scaffolding: temporary support provided by teacher/peer to help learner cross ZPD.  l   • Learning is social — language and interaction drive cognitive development.  Erikson (Psychosocial Development):   8 stages — most tested: Trust vs Mistrust (infancy), Industry vs Inferiority (school age 6-12).  Kohlberg (Moral Development):   3 levels: Pre-conventional, Conventional, Post-conventional.  Pavlov:   Classical conditioning: stimulus-response learning. |   Skinner:   Operant conditioning: reinforcement/punishment.  5.3 Teaching Methodologies
FGEI EST BPS-14 Preparation Package   Page 12  Lecture Method:   Teacher-centered; efficient for large groups; passive learners.  Inquiry-Based Learning:   Students ask questions and investigate; promotes critical thinking; Constructivist.  Cooperative Learning:   Students work in small groups; develops social skills (Jigsaw, Think-Pair-Share, STAD).  Problem-Based Learning (PBL):   Real-world problems as starting point; learner-centered; develops analytical skills.  Project-Based Learning:   Extended inquiry resulting in a product/presentation; authentic assessment.  Demonstration Method:   Teacher shows; students observe then practice; best for procedural skills.  Discussion Method:   Two-way communication; develops higher-order thinking and communication skills.  Discovery Learning (Bruner):   Learners construct knowledge through exploration and discovery.  5.4 Types of Assessment  Formative Assessment:   Ongoing assessment DURING learning (quizzes, exit tickets, observation). Improves learning.  Summative Assessment:   Assessment AT THE END of instruction (final exam, term paper). Measures achievement.  Diagnostic Assessment:   Before instruction — identifies prior knowledge and gaps (pre-test).  Norm-Referenced:   Compares student to peers (e.g., national tests, IQ tests).  Criterion-Referenced:   Compares student against a fixed standard (e.g., driving test, passing mark of 50%).  Authentic Assessment:   Real-world tasks: portfolios, presentations, projects.  Portfolio Assessment:   Collection of student work over time showing growth and reflection.  5.5 Classroom Management & Key Concepts  Motivation theories:   Maslow's Hierarchy (Physiological → Safety → Love → Esteem → Self-Actualization) | Intrinsic vs Extrinsic motivation  Individual differences:   Students differ in learning styles, pace, background, ability. Differentiated instruction addresses this.  Inclusive education:   Teaching ALL students in the same environment regardless of ability or disability.  IEP:   Individualized Education Program — customized plan for students with special needs.  Multiple Intelligences (Gardner):   Linguistic, Logical-Mathematical, Spatial, Musical, Bodily-Kinesthetic, Interpersonal, Intrapersonal, Naturalist.  Transfer of learning:   Positive transfer: prior learning helps new learning. Negative: prior learning interferes.  Curriculum types:   Formal (written/official), Informal (hidden curriculum), Non-formal (outside school).
